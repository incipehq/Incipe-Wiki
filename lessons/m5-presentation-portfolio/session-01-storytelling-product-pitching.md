### Key words

| Word | Plain meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Pitch** | A short talk that convinces listeners your problem matters and your solution is worth backing. |
| **Hook** | The first sentence or two, made to grab attention. |
| **Impact** | What changes for people because your product exists. |
| **Hero's Journey** | A story pattern found in many films and books: a hero meets a problem, gets help, overcomes it and is changed. |
| **Guide** | In the Hero's Journey, the wise helper who gives the hero a plan — in a pitch, that's your product. |
| **Call to action** | The last line: what you want the listeners to do next. |

## 1. Why stories beat feature lists

**Analogy: two ways to recommend a film.** "It's 2 hours 10 minutes, shot in 4K, with a 120-person crew" — nobody cares. "A girl loses her dog in a storm and crosses the whole city to find it" — now you want to watch. Facts tell; stories sell.

A product pitch works the same way. Listeners remember a person with a problem long after they've forgotten your sensor's name. The features still matter — they just go *inside* the story.

## 2. The pitch structure: Problem → Solution → Technology → Impact

The curriculum's four-part structure, in order:

| Part | Question it answers | Smart Garden example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Problem** | Who is struggling, and with what? | "Students who travel for competitions come back to dead plants." |
| **Solution** | What does your product do about it, in one sentence? | "A pot that waters itself, only when the soil is actually dry." |
| **Technology** | How does it work — simply? | "A probe measures how wet the soil is; the board waters below 30 % and stops above 60 %." |
| **Impact** | What changes, and how do you know? | "In our test, [the plant stayed healthy for 7 days away]. [3] of the [5] students we interviewed asked for one." |

**Keep the order.** If you start with technology, listeners don't know yet *why* they should care about it.

**Impact needs evidence you collected**, not wishes. "Millions will love it" proves nothing; "[3] of [5] people we interviewed lose a plant every term" does. Replace every `[ ]` with your own real numbers from your interviews and tests — never invent one.

## 3. The Hero's Journey: your user is the hero

**Analogy: a classic adventure film.** An ordinary person, a problem, a wise guide with a plan, a hard moment, a victory, a changed life. Your pitch can follow the same path — with one important twist: **the hero is your user, not you and not your product.** Your product is the **guide** — the helper with the plan.

| Beat | In a film | In your pitch (Smart Garden) | Pitch part |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Ordinary world | The hero's normal life | Mei, a student, loves her desk plants and competes in robotics. | Problem |
| 2. The problem | Something goes wrong | Every competition weekend, her plants dry out. Three died this term. | Problem |
| 3. Meets the guide | A wise helper appears | The Smart Garden: it checks the soil and waters only when needed. | Solution |
| 4. The plan | The guide explains how | A soil probe, two thresholds and a pump — no flooding, no forgetting. | Technology |
| 5. The victory | The hero wins the battle | Mei spends a week at the finals; the plant is fine when she returns. | Impact |
| 6. A changed world | Life is better now | She travels without worrying — and her teammates want one too. | Impact |

Telling it this way, the listeners care about Mei, so they care about what helped her.

> Mei is a made-up user for this example. In your pitch, build your hero from the user profile and the real interviews in [M4 Session 2](/academy/m4-ideation/02-product-market-fit-validation) — and say if they're a composite of several people you spoke to.

**Try it (3 min).** Fill in the six beats for your own project, one sentence each. Who is your hero?

## 4. Workshop: a 5-minute pitch script

The curriculum task: *draft a 5-minute pitch script for your Smart Garden or Game Console.* It grows out of your 3-minute problem pitch from M4 — now with the solution, the technology and the impact in full.

| Part | Time | What to say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 30 s | Open with your hero in their ordinary world (beat 1), or a surprising moment from your interviews. |
| Problem | 60 s | Beat 2: the problem, who has it, and what your interviews found. |
| Solution | 60 s | Beat 3: your product in one sentence, then what it does for the hero. Show it or a photo. |
| Technology | 75 s | Beat 4: the plan, in plain words — what it senses, what it decides, what it does. Your flowchart from M4 helps. |
| Impact | 45 s | Beats 5 and 6: what changed in your tests, with real numbers. |
| Close | 30 s | A call to action: "Try it at our stand", "We're looking for five testers". |

That adds up to exactly 5 minutes.

### Write it

| Step | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Pick your hero and write the six beats (the Try it). |
| 2 | Write the script in the six parts above, in full sentences — the way you'll *say* it, not bullet points. |
| 3 | Read it aloud with a stopwatch. Mark where each part ends. |
| 4 | Too long? Cut technical detail and extra features first — never the problem or the hero. |
| 5 | Swap scripts with a partner. They underline one sentence they'll remember tomorrow and circle one they didn't understand. Fix the circled one. |

> **AI as an editor.** Paste your script into the Workspace AI chat and ask: "Point out any sentence where the product, not the user, is the hero, and suggest a rewrite. Don't add any facts or numbers I haven't given you." Check every suggestion — the AI must not invent results for your Impact part.

**Your user.** Your product is the guide that helps them — not the hero.

<details>

</details>

</details>

## Recap

1. Stories beat feature lists: people remember a person with a problem.
2. Structure the pitch Problem → Solution → Technology → Impact, and back the impact with real numbers you collected.
3. In the Hero's Journey, your user is the hero and your product is the guide — write the 5-minute script that way, then cut detail, not the story.
